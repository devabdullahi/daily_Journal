# Planner color rules

Source: [Mastering Color Theory for Oil Painters](https://www.color2oilpaint.com/tutorials/oil-painting/mastering-color-theory-for-oil-painters) (color2oilpaint), added at the user's request on 2026-10-28. The engine only has historical tubes (no cadmiums, alizarin or phthalo), so each rule is mapped to the engine's tube box.

| # | Rule (from the guide) | In the engine's tube box |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mix clean secondaries from primaries that lean toward that secondary | Greens: Prussian blue (cool blue) + chrome yellow; avoid red earth/vermilion in greens except as a muting tip |
| 2 | Bright greens from a cool blue + a cool yellow | Prussian blue + chrome yellow (+ lead white for sunlit greens) |
| 3 | Oranges from a warm red + a warm yellow | vermilion + chrome yellow |
| 4 | Purples from a cool red + a warm blue | No cool red in the box: avoid bright purples; use cobalt blue + red earth + lead white for muted violets |
| 5 | Mute a color with a **tip** of its complement and stop early | Greens: a tip of red earth (≈2–5% of the pile); blues: a tip of yellow ochre or red earth; never more |
| 6 | Mute fast with earth tones | yellow ochre, red earth, raw umber |
| 7 | Don't reach for black by reflex; darken with a dark chromatic mix | raw umber + cobalt blue or Prussian blue instead of bone black, unless black is a deliberate choice |
| 8 | Yellow + black gives olive; orange + black gives muted brown | Never darken chrome yellow or vermilion mixes with bone black |
| 9 | Lots of white cools warm colors and makes reds chalky: add back a touch of yellow or orange | Light pinks and creams get a touch of chrome yellow (≈1–2%) |
| 10 | A neutral reads warm beside blue and cool beside orange (simultaneous contrast) | Under warm light, a neutral grey shadow already reads cool; don't over-blue it |
| 11 | Judge a mix where it sits on the canvas, not on the palette | Validation renders the passage in place (the gate's checks run on the render) |
| 12 | Values before color | The focal object gets the strongest value contrast against its surroundings |

## Rules learned from our own test paintings
- **Reserve space:** paint a foreground object on clean canvas (subtract its mask from background passes), never over wet background paint. The ice cream turned green when painted over wet grass.
- **Light goes into the passage,** not on top: grade the wall or desk warmer toward the lamp instead of glazing yellow over it. Strong tubes (chrome yellow) can't be used as see-through glazes.
- **Never mix complementaries in one passage** (blue sky into orange sunset): use separate bands with soft-masked overlaps.
- **Palette limit:** at most 16 piles; scrape finished passages' piles before new ones.
- **Isolated regular marks read as objects** (posts, sticks): avoid them unless an object is meant.
